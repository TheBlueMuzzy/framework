# Where are you starting? — the opening and the stage intros (F225 task 1)

Written 2026-11-09 by Claude, from Muzzy's live-look notes (SPRINT Notes 17:50). **Critical thinking, not outside research**: no sources were read for this draft. A research pass (how design courses, game jams and studio greenlight processes describe where an idea stands) can check it if Muzzy wants.

## What Muzzy asked for
- A self-identifying, self-selecting opening that puts the designer in at any stage of the same pipeline, including sending a full idea back through Discover.
- The engine reads what's shared and sets the real entry point, even when the designer's own label is off.
- No different behaviour by experience level. An experienced designer simply enters later.
- "I don't know where to start" is Discover's spark: the experience question already prompts the seed.
- Every stage opens with a SHORT paragraph: which stage, its goal, what you'll do.

## The rules for the opening
1. **At most two taps before real work.** One question, a second one only when something already exists, then the engine's proposal.
2. **The labels describe the game, never the person.** Nobody is asked "how experienced are you?".
3. **The engine proposes, the creative confirms.** One line of why, then "Start there" first; "Start earlier" and "Start somewhere else" always open.
4. **A later start never blocks.** When a stage needs something an earlier stage makes (Design needs the concept's pillars), the engine runs only those missing steps as a short catch-up, and their answers come from the material when it has them (one tap to keep).
5. **Nothing the material says is asked again.** It goes onto the shelves marked "from your material" and shows as "already chosen" for one-tap keep.

## Question 1 — "Where's your game right now?"
| Option | Then | Usually enters at |
|---|---|---|
| Nothing yet: I want to make something but don't know what | — | Discover · Spark (the experience question is the seed) |
| A spark: a theme, a mechanism, a feeling or a "what if" | type it | Discover · Spark, pre-filled |
| A concept I can describe in a few sentences | type or paste it | read → proposed |
| Something written: notes, a pitch, a rulebook or a GDD | point at files or paste | read → proposed |
| A prototype I've played (paper or digital) | describe it + any playtest notes | read → proposed (often Design) |

## Question 2 (only after the last three) — "What do you want from this pass?"
| Option | Stage |
|---|---|
| Open it up: find more options before choosing | Discover (a full idea can go back through it) |
| Pin it down: choose what it's made of and what's in the first release | Define |
| Work it out: rules, systems, playtests | Design |
| Build it | Develop |

## The proposal (one screen)
- **What I read:** 2–3 lines in the creative's words.
- **What it already covers,** stage by stage: covered / partly / not yet (a gap map, one line per stage).
- **Where I'd start you:** stage and step, with one line of why. If their Question 2 answer is later than the material supports: "Design needs pillars and a first-release scope; I'll ask those two in a short catch-up first."
- Options: "Start there" · "Start earlier: <the earlier stage>" · "Somewhere else".

## The stage intros (shown once, at a stage's first step; 2–3 sentences) — Muzzy's wording 2026-11-09
Muzzy: Discover collects the MATERIAL for building blocks (before blocks exist) · Define MAKES the blocks from it · Design ASSEMBLES them (cutting some, tweaking others) · Develop makes the PRODUCTION MODEL from the blueprints · Release lets the PUBLIC experience it.
- **Discover:** We're in Discover: gathering the raw material for the game — how it should feel, its theme, things you might do in it, games like it. Nothing is shaped or chosen yet. A few questions at a time, research in between, and you can wrap up whenever you have enough.
- **Define:** We're in Define: turning that material into the game's building blocks — the feelings that matter, a 3–5 line story, the pillars, the core verbs, and what goes in the first release. It ends with one page, the concept.
- **Design:** We're in Design: assembling the blocks in whatever way feels right — cutting some, tweaking others — into rules and systems, tested with sketches and paper playtests. It ends with the blueprint: the full design and the build plan.
- **Develop:** We're in Develop: building the production model from the blueprint, in short sprints you play and approve one piece at a time.
- **Release:** We're in Release: letting the public start experiencing it — final checks, polish, and a live link.

## Weighed
- **Why it's this way now:** every run starts at Discover's spark because Discover was the first pipeline built; nothing else existed to enter.
- **Long term:** one pipeline per stage and one entry step keeps "the same way every time"; entering later is a position in it, not a new path to maintain.
- **Standards:** design courses and studio greenlights judge an idea by what exists (a pitch, a prototype, a design doc), not by who made it. *Not checked against sources in this draft.*
- **His goals:** an experienced designer gets past the beginner questions in two taps; a beginner gets the full Discover; both run the same system.
- **The case against:**
  1. Two questions plus a proposal is still three screens before work. Answer: the proposal is the work (it reads their material), and Question 2 is skipped when nothing exists.
  2. Entering late hides gaps. Answer: the gap map shows them, and the catch-up asks only what the entered stage needs.
  3. The engine overriding the label could feel presumptuous. Answer: it proposes with a one-line why, and "Start there" is the creative's tap.
  4. Reading Google Docs or PDFs needs more than a file read. Answer: the first version takes files in the folder or pasted text; Drive links come later.
