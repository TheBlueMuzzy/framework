# BMUZ stages — Ideate · Define · Design · Develop · Deliver · Release (+ Research anywhere)

Decided by Muzzy 2026-11-10: "Ideate > Define > Design > Develop > Deliver > Release with research being callable anywhere." Replaces the Double Diamond start (VISION V48's five stages, 2026-11-09). Why: the Double Diamond begins by researching someone else's problem; a game begins from the creative's idea and is found by shaping, sketching and playing. Evidence: discover-live-review-2026-11-09.md (research fed one useful question in a 63-minute Discover; 15 of 33 questions fed nothing) and design-gaps-evidence.md (51 rework cases from frame, rules and look gaps nobody asked or sketched, none from missing research).

## The stages (the game's state at each one; Muzzy's material → blocks wording)
| Stage | Meaning | Asks | Ends with |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Ideate** | gather the material: the loose idea, captured | "show me what you have" (all of it read, Drive too); then, per need, *what's already fixed · what ideas you have · what's open*. Never "which one?" | "The game so far" written back: fixed / ideas / open per need, sharper than what the creative walked in with |
| **Define** | make the blocks: the tight concept | "which one is it?" per need, one answer each | GDD Part 1, the one-page concept, locked |
| **Design** | assemble the blocks: cut some, tweak others | rules, systems, screens worked out; sketches and paper play settle the risky parts | the full GDD, then the build plan (TDD + feature map, checked against the GDD) |
| **Develop** | the production model | sprints; each piece played and approved | the game built to the plan |
| **Deliver** | *to confirm with Muzzy:* the finished product made ready to hand over — final checks, polish, versions, builds per platform, store submission | | a ready build per platform |
| **Release** | the public experiences it | *to confirm:* launch, the live link, players' first reactions | the game in players' hands |

## Research — callable from any stage
Sourced research (the flow built for F217, every claim with a working source), started when it's useful, never by default. Triggers: a creative block · a worry about being unique · something we've never built · a rule that doesn't work · something that needs improving · a risk to test · "what goes wrong in games like this" · prior art before locking the concept (Define offers it once). Findings come back as "here's what I found — does it change anything?", never as a menu.

## The needs (the spine of Ideate, Define and Design)
1 core loop in verbs · 2 things in play + their states · 3 rules (how it ends, how it scores) · 4 decisions + why they're hard (the real skill) · 5 feel target + how we'll know · 6 what changes over time · 7 frame (platform, which way up, players, online incl. leaving/joining, session length, controls) · 8 smallest version worth playing · 9 look and sound direction — plus from the evidence: opponents (AI) · who sees what, when · screens and options · names · what story beats change in play · what the player must see (HUD) · teaching. Ideate gathers material on the first nine; Define chooses them; Design settles the rest (screens, HUD, names, teaching last), each with a sketch before the full build.

## Order of asking (from the rework evidence)
Frame first (it got more expensive with every screen built on it) → rules' shape and the real skill before any code → look, HUD and feel by sketch before the full build → exact numbers late → teaching last → stand-ins before any polish hunt.
